# ASVspoof 2019 LA

---

## 1. Summary

- ASVspoof 2019 LA is a set of short speech recordings used to train and test programs (detectors) that decide whether a voice is **real or generated by a computer**.
- **Real recordings** (called *bona fide*) come from VCTK – a public collection of recordings of English speakers reading sentences.
- **Fake recordings** (called *spoof*) were made from them with 17 different programs. Some turn written text into speech (TTS), others change one person's voice so that it sounds like another person (VC).
- **"LA" (logical access)** means that the fake audio is given to the system directly as a file – it is not played through a loudspeaker and recorded again with a microphone.
- The recordings are split into three parts: **train** (to teach a detector), **dev** (to tune it) and **eval** (final test). The eval part also contains types of fakes that never appear in train or dev.
- It is the most widely used dataset in this field. The detectors used in this project (AASIST, RawNet2 and the wav2vec 2.0-based detector) were trained on it.

  ```
  ├── ASVspoof2019_LA_eval            # audio files of the eval part
  ├── ASVspoof2019_LA_train           # audio files of the train part
  └── README.LA.txt                   # detailed description of the files in LA/
  ```

Most important parts of `README.txt`, quoted directly:

**What is new in 2019:**

> "The ASVspoof 2019 challenge extends the previous challenge in several directions. The 2019 edition is the first to focus on countermeasures for all three major attack types, namely those stemming from TTS, VC and replay spoofing attacks."

**LA and PA, speakers and splits:**

> "The ASVspoof 2019 database encompasses two partitions for the assessment of logical access (LA) and physical access (PA) scenarios. Both are derived from the VCTK base corpus which includes speech data captured from 107 speakers (46 males, 61 females). Both LA and PA databases are themselves partitioned into three datasets, namely training, development and evaluation which comprise the speech from 20 (8 male, 12 female), 10 (4 male, 6 female) and 48 (21 male, 27 female) speakers respectively. The three partitions are disjoint in terms of speakers, and the recording conditions for all source data are identical. While the training and development sets contain spoofing attacks generated with the same algorithms/conditions (designated as known attacks), the evaluation set also contains attacks generated with different algorithms/conditions (designated as unknown attacks)."

**Training, development and evaluation data:**

> "Training and development data for the LA scenario are included in 'ASVspoof2019_LA_train' 'ASVspoof2019_LA_dev'. Training dataset contains audio files with known ground-truth, which can be used to train systems to distinguish genuine and spoofed speech. The development dataset contains audio files with known ground-truth which can be used for the development of spoofing detection algorithms."

> "Evaluation data for LA and PA are available in 'ASVspoof2019_LA_eval' and 'ASVspoof2019_PA_eval', respectively."

**Labels:**

> "Protocol and keys are available in 'ASVspoof2019_LA_{cm,asv}_protocols' and ASVspoof2019_PA_{cm,asv}_protocols, respectively."

**Licence** (details in section 5.2):

> "You are free to use this database under Open Data Commons Attribution License (ODC-By)."

### 5.3 `asvspoof2019_evaluation_plan.pdf`

Official technical description of the challenge (version 0.4, 15 January 2019):

- **Section 3 – Logical access data:** how the bona fide and spoofed speech was created, training / development / evaluation subsets, known vs unknown attacks.
- **Section 4 – Physical access data:** replay simulation (not used in this project).
- **Section 5 – Performance measures:**
  - primary metric: minimum normalised **t-DCF** (combines the countermeasure with an ASV system);
  - secondary metric: **EER** – recommended for fake audio detection without an ASV system, i.e. our use case.
  - Score convention: **a high score means bona fide, a low score means spoof.**
- **Section 6 – Baselines:** LFCC-GMM and CQCC-GMM.
- **Section 7 – Evaluation rules:** official protocol files (Table 5); during the challenge no external data or pretrained models were allowed.
- **Section 8 – Registration and submission** (historical, no longer relevant).

## 7. Dataset content: recordings and splits

_What is inside `LA.zip`: what kind of recordings the dataset contains and how they are divided into parts. Based on the main paper (pages 3–6: sections 2.1–2.3, Figure 2 and Table 1)._

**Bona fide speech:** VCTK corpus, 107 speakers in total (46 male, 61 female), recorded in a hemi-anechoic chamber (a room without echo) at 96 kHz and downsampled to 16 kHz for ASVspoof 2019.

**Spoofed speech:** 17 different TTS / VC algorithms, labelled **A01–A19** in the protocol files. There are 19 labels but only 17 algorithms, because A16 uses the same algorithm as A04, and A19 the same as A06.

| Attacks | Used in | Known / unknown | Type | Source in the main paper |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A01–A06 | train, dev | known | 4 TTS (A01–A04), 2 VC (A05–A06) | p. 6, Table 1 and section 2.2.2 text below Table 1 ("two VC and four TTS systems") |
| A07–A15, A17, A18 | eval | unknown (11 attacks) | 6 TTS (A07–A12), 3 hybrid TTS-VC (A13–A15), 2 VC (A17–A18) | p. 6, Table 1 ("Input" column: Text = TTS, Speech (TTS) = hybrid, Speech (human) = VC) and text below it ("eleven unknown spoofing systems") |
| A16, A19 | eval | known (same algorithms as A04 and A06) | 1 TTS (A16), 1 VC (A19) | p. 6, Table 1 caption and text below Table 1 ("A16 and A19 are the known reference systems") |

**Splits** (speakers do not overlap between splits):

| Split | Speakers | Bona fide | Spoof | Total | Protocol file | Source in the main paper |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| train | 20 (12 F, 8 M) | 2,580 | 22,800 | 25,380 | `ASVspoof2019.LA.cm.train.trn.txt` | speakers: p. 3, section 2.1 and p. 4, Figure 2 (top part); counts: p. 5, section 2.3 and p. 4, Figure 2 (#5, #7) |
| dev | 20: 10 target (6 F, 4 M) + 10 non-target (6 F, 4 M) | 2,548 | 22,296 | 24,844 | `ASVspoof2019.LA.cm.dev.trl.txt` | speakers: p. 3, section 2.1 and p. 4, Figure 2 (top part); counts: p. 5, section 2.3 and p. 4, Figure 2 (#6, #8) |
| eval | 67: 48 target (27 F, 21 M) + 19 non-target (10 F, 9 M) | 7,355 | 63,882 | 71,237 | `ASVspoof2019.LA.cm.eval.trl.txt` | speakers: p. 3, section 2.1 and p. 4, Figure 2 (top part); counts: p. 5, section 2.3 and p. 4, Figure 2 (#9, #10) |

Page numbers refer to the arXiv PDF of the main paper (https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.01601). Protocol file names come from the evaluation plan (Table 5).

**ASV enrollment recordings (audio files without real / fake labels):**

The dev and eval audio folders contain more files than there are rows in the label files. The extra files are *enrollment* recordings: recordings used to register a person's voice in the speaker verification (ASV) system. They belong to the ASV task only, so they have no `bonafide` / `spoof` label and are skipped in this project.

- `README.txt`, point 3: *"Dev and eval enrollment data for ASV are available in 'ASVspoof2019_{LA,PA}_dev' and 'ASVspoof2019_{LA,PA}_eval', respectively."*
- Main paper, p. 3, section 2.1 (and p. 4, Figure 2, boxes #1 and #2): *"there are 11 enrollment utterances for female speakers and 19 utterances for male speakers for both development and evaluation partitions"*.
- The number of enrollment recordings is not given directly, but it follows from the number of target speakers:

| Split | Female target speakers × 11 | Male target speakers × 19 | Enrollment recordings | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| train | – | – | 0 | – |
| dev | 6 × 11 = 66 | 4 × 19 = 76 | **142** | main paper, p. 3, section 2.1 and p. 4, Figure 2 |
| eval | 27 × 11 = 297 | 21 × 19 = 399 | **696** | main paper, p. 3, section 2.1 and p. 4, Figure 2 |

Where each kind of file is listed:

| Files | Listed in `ASVspoof2019_LA_cm_protocols` (real / fake labels) | Listed in `ASVspoof2019_LA_asv_protocols` |
|---|---|---|
| train recordings (25,380) | yes | no |
| dev and eval test recordings (24,844 and 71,237) | yes | yes – the same files, in the trial lists (`*.trl.txt`) |
| dev and eval enrollment recordings (142 and 696) | **no** | yes – only in the enrollment lists (`*.trn.txt`) |

## 8. Technical properties of the audio files


| Property | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Original recordings | VCTK corpus: one omni-directional microphone (DPA 4035), hemi-anechoic chamber (a room without echo), 96 kHz, 24 bits; VCTK itself is distributed at 48 kHz, 16 bits | main paper, p. 3, section 2.1; VCTK 0.80 `README.txt`, Overview |
| Sample rate | 16 kHz (downsampled from 96 kHz) | main paper, p. 3, section 2.1; `README.LA.txt`, section 2 |
| Bit depth | 16 bits per sample | main paper, p. 3, section 2.1; `README.LA.txt`, section 2 |
| File format | FLAC (lossless compression – smaller files, identical sound); file names `LA_T_*.flac` (train), `LA_D_*.flac` (dev), `LA_E_*.flac` (eval) | `README.LA.txt`, section 2 (not stated in the main paper) |
| Microphone / room effects | none – in LA, fake speech goes to the system "without convolutive acoustic propagation or microphone effects" | main paper, p. 4, section 2.2.1 |
| Background noise | "no significant channel or background noise effects" in the bona fide (VCTK) recordings | evaluation plan, p. 3, section 3 (not stated in the main paper) |
| Recording conditions across splits | identical for train, dev and eval | `README.txt`, Overview (not stated in the main paper) |
| Typical duration | "in the order of one to two seconds" per recording | evaluation plan, p. 3, Table 1 caption (not stated in the main paper) |
| Data cleaning by the authors | low-quality recordings were removed ("pruning of low-quality recordings") | main paper, p. 3, section 2.1 |
| Silence trimming / loudness normalisation by the authors | VCTK recordings were "manually end-pointed" (start and end trimmed by hand) by the VCTK authors. No further silence trimming or loudness normalisation is described for ASVspoof 2019 | VCTK 0.80 `README.txt`, Overview; not mentioned in the main paper, `README.txt`, `README.LA.txt` or the evaluation plan |

`README.LA.txt` is inside `LA.zip` (folder `LA/`). Page numbers refer to the arXiv PDF of the main paper and to the evaluation plan PDF.

## 9. Protocol (label) files

_Files that say which recording is bona fide and which is spoofed. Without them EER cannot be computed. Based on `README.LA.txt`, section 3 (inside `LA.zip`)._

Located in `LA/ASVspoof2019_LA_cm_protocols/`:

| File | Content |
|---|---|
| `ASVspoof2019.LA.cm.train.trn.txt` | list of training files |
| `ASVspoof2019.LA.cm.dev.trl.txt` | list of development files |
| `ASVspoof2019.LA.cm.eval.trl.txt` | list of evaluation files |

One line per audio file, five columns separated by spaces. Example lines from `ASVspoof2019.LA.cm.train.trn.txt`:

```
LA_0079 LA_T_1138215 - - bonafide
LA_0079 LA_T_1004644 - A01 spoof
```

| # | Column | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `SPEAKER_ID` | ID of the speaker | `LA_0079` |
| 2 | `AUDIO_FILE_NAME` | name of the audio file (without `.flac`) | `LA_T_1138215` |
| 3 | `-` | **not used in LA** – always `-`. It exists only so that LA files have the same structure as PA files, where this column is used | `-` |
| 4 | `SYSTEM_ID` | attack ID (`A01`–`A19`) for spoofed speech, `-` for bona fide speech | `A01` / `-` |
| 5 | `KEY` | label: `bonafide` (real) or `spoof` (fake) | `spoof` |
